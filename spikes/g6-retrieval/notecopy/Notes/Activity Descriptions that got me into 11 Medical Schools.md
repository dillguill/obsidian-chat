## **Medical School Activity Descriptions: The "Three-Part" Formula**

### **The Structure**

To avoid making your activity descriptions feel like a "boring resume," use this three-part breakdown for each entry:

1. **The Overview (1-2 sentences):** A high-level summary of your specific role and core responsibilities.
    
2. **The Narrative/Story (2-3 sentences):** An anecdote or specific interaction (e.g., connecting with a patient like "Mel" or "Martha"). This humanizes the experience and demonstrates your impact in a real-world setting.
    
3. **The Reflection (Final sentence):** Focus on what you learned or how it shaped your outlook.
    
    - **Pro Tip:** This does _not_ have to explicitly state "this is why I'll be a good doctor." It just needs to relate to your future self and professional growth.
        

### **Examples from the Video**

#### **1. Research Assistant**

- **Action:** Conducted participant interviews, ran fMRI tasks, and collected biospecimens.
    
- **Story:** Connected with a teenager ("Mel") struggling with depression who lacked access to affordable care.
    
- **Reflection:** Motivated to curate local mental health resources and dismantle barriers to care for underserved populations.
    

#### **2. Pharmacy Technician**

- **Action:** Filled prescriptions and coordinated with physicians to verify orders.
    
- **Story:** Helped a patient ("Martha") who was frustrated with insurance/refill delays by proactively contacting her physician.
    
- **Reflection:** Motivated to provide tailored patient care, addressing individual needs with a proactive and compassionate approach.
    

### **Key Takeaways**

- **Avoid "Listing":** Don't just list tasks; show how you performed them.
    
- **Human Connection:** Use specific (anonymized) names or situations to make the entry memorable.
    
- **Future-Oriented:** The reflection should bridge the gap between your past experience and your future trajectory.
